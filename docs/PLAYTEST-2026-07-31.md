## B. THE RE-SIGNING WINDOW — the second epic  ✅ BUILT

*"Thats a big one to handle."* Currently one screen with instant accepts.

| # | Finding | Bar |
|---|---------|-----|
| B1 | **Re-signing is a single screen with instant accepts** — should be the real window, playing out **over time**: offers are considered, not answered on the spot. | B3.2 |
| B2 | **No qualifying offers for our own RFAs** | B3.2 |
| B3 | **Offer sheets not handled in the window** (the async engine exists — #168/#183 — but the window doesn't surface it) | B3.2 |
| B4 | **Too easy to sign a player** by offering the same AAV he asked for but more years. Term has to cost something. | B3.2 |

**Shipped 2026-07-31.** The re-sign stage is now a 4-day June window
(`OffseasonState.resignDay`), each Continue another day before July 1.

- **B1** — `submitResignOffer` tables terms into an async queue
  (`Career.resignOffers`, the same shape as `faPendingOffers` / `pendingOfferSheets`
  / `pendingTrades`); the camp answers a day or two later with **accept**, a
  **priced counter** you can sign as written (`acceptResignCounter`), a
  **refusal** that burns patience, or a **walk-out** when the patience is gone.
  Decision logic lives in the pure `src/engine/league/resignWindow.ts`.
- **B2** — qualifying offers on the real CBA ladder (`qualifyingOffer`, 110/105/100%
  of prior salary). Obvious keepers are auto-tendered; the bubble is a real
  choice. Tendering keeps his rights (and exposes him to offer sheets and
  arbitration); walking away makes him unrestricted for nothing. A depth RFA can
  simply *take* the QO as a one-year deal.
- **B3** — rival offer sheets only land on **qualified** RFAs, arrive in two
  waves inside the window, and run a live match clock (`matchDaysLeft`). An
  unanswered sheet resolves itself instead of sitting inert until the stage ends.
- **B4** — `termPriceMultiplier` / `termSecurityScore` in `contracts.ts`. Extra
  years past the ask are a concession the CLUB is buying, so they raise the AAV
  he needs to see (more for bought UFA years, more again past 30). Both the
  async queue and the live negotiation table (`offerValue`) price through it, and
  the offer builder shows the bill per term. Meeting the ask exactly is still
  exactly 1.0 — the calibration didn't move.

Everything above is additive on `CareerSnapshot` (`resignOffers`,
`resignPatience`, `qualifyingOffers`, `offseason.resignDay`) and round-trips
through save/load. **Not done:** a sit-down in the negotiation screen still ends
in a handshake the moment the agent says yes — by design (docs/DEPTH §1.4), the
async queue is the surface that models the clock.

### D1 — the end-to-end pass (done)

**Latency — profiled, not guessed.** onnxruntime-web runs single-threaded and
un-proxied on the renderer's *main* thread, so answering the phone synthesised
the whole message before a sound came out, with the UI frozen for the duration.
Measured on the **faster** native-CPU backend (WASM is slower still):

| call | words | whole message | first sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| player concern | 14 | 2.09 s | 0.67 s |
| owner directive | 39 | 4.36 s | 1.13 s |
| blockbuster GM | 49 | 4.80 s | 1.43 s |

(Absolute values move with machine load — a re-run on a busy box gave 5.6/11.8/15.1 s
against 2.5/4.3/7.7 s. The ratio is the point: **most of the wait is the part of
the message you haven't heard yet.**)

Three fixes, in order of effect:
1. **Prewarm while it rings.** The handset rings for ~5 s before you click it —
   that idle window now synthesises the opening line into the clip cache, so
   Answer is a cache hit.
2. **Chunked streaming synthesis** (`chunkForSpeech`): playback starts after the
   first chunk and each later chunk is synthesised while the previous one plays,
   so the main thread blocks in short slices instead of one long one.
3. **Paint before speaking** — the card flips to "on the line" before synthesis
   is allowed to start, and a hang-up cancels a not-yet-started line.

**Logic — root cause: the phone was voicing the inbox's prose.** Every string it
spoke was written for a *card*: third-person narration, stage directions and UI
consequence hints. The owner rang to say "the owner is looking at the balance
sheet… refusing tests his patience"; a club rang as "— front office" to read out
an asset list; a winger rang to narrate himself closing your office door. Fixed
by splitting **what is written** from **what is said**:

| caller | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | card body, third person + hints | authored first-person directive per template |
| Rival GM | "NSH are after X. On the table: …" | the GM **by name**, first person, naming who he wants |
| Player concern | first person (already right) | unchanged |
| Authored dilemma | the whole narrated scene, read aloud | only the dialogue inside it, in the voice of whoever actually says it |

And the rule that decides whether it rings at all: **the phone rings only when
somebody is genuinely speaking.** Of the 21 authored dilemmas, 15 contain a real
spoken line and ring; 6 are pure narration ("Two clubs have called about him in
as many days…") and now stay in the office, which is where their own news item
already pointed. The one the GM praised — a shopped man asking to stay — still
rings, saying exactly the right thing.

Two more real bugs fixed in passing: the "already rung" set was keyed on
per-career counters (`i0`, `o1`, …) that **restart every new career**, so a fresh
game inherited the last one's suppressions; and answering, then having a
higher-priority call arrive, left the card claiming a man was "on the line" who
had never rung and never spoke. Hanging up also now buys 45 s of quiet instead of
instantly ringing the next item in the queue.

### D2 — neural voice on by default (done)

The app-wide auto-download was already correct, and is **verified working in the
production bundle** (`out/`, `loadFile` → `file://`, sandbox on): on a clean
profile with nothing ever clicked, the renderer logs `[voice] neural voices ready`
and leaves `transformers-cache` + `kokoro-voices` behind. What was broken:

* `MatchViewer` spoke through its **own** `new Announcer()` — an instance the
  neural engine is never attached to — behind its **own** default-off preference
  (`hockeyKokoroEnabled`) and its own "Enhanced voice" button. Match commentary,
  the loudest voice in the game, was therefore robotic until you found and pressed
  that button. It now speaks through the one shared announcer, and its toggle is
  the app-wide opt-**out**.
* The background loader latched off after a **single** failure, so one bad network
  moment at launch cost neural voices for the whole session — and left Settings
  showing a button you had to press. It now retries with backoff (30 s → 15 min),
  and Settings reports status rather than demanding a click.

### H1–H3 — CLOSED 2026-07-31 (2 critical + 4 major → 0 + 0 on the same seed)

Three separate holes, one theme: an offer was treated as valid because it hadn't
expired yet, and the GM's own deals were the one trade path nobody policed.

- **H1 — validity ≠ expiry.** `offerDeadReason()` asks whether every asset an
  offer names is still where it was (your man on your NHL roster, their man on
  theirs, both picks still owned); `pruneDeadOffers()` retires the ones that
  aren't, with a call from the GM who tabled it ("PIT pull their offer — Sidney
  Crosby is no longer on your NHL roster") so the desk clearing itself reads as
  news. It runs on every advance and before every read of the offer list.
- **H2 — a deal you can't complete is not an offer.** Both generators checked
  only the PARTNER's cap; they now check the user's the same way, so the illegal
  offer is never tabled. Room can still vanish after it's tabled, so
  `TradeOfferView.blockedReason` carries the reason, both screens grey out Accept
  and print it, and the AGM's read (`assessTrade`) now runs the identical
  arithmetic — he can't bless a deal the engine will refuse.
- **H3 — the remaining uncapped path was `executeUserTrade`.** Every INCOMING
  offer was cap-guarded; the trades the GM proposed himself went propose → sleep
  on it → execute with no cap check at all. That is what put a club $6.5M over
  the ceiling with Gap #4's `callUp` guard already in place. Now the deal falls
  through and says why (the caller already reported that as news), and
  `proposeTrade` refuses up front rather than two days later. The emergency
  recall pass is untouched — it still eats an overage to ice a legal lineup, as
  `callUpCap.test.ts` pins.
- **And a fourth, found by the fix.** With H1/H2 closed the autopilot surfaced
  one more throw: two clubs bidding for the same player, one deal taken, the
  other offer accepted off a stale list. `acceptTrade` no longer throws on that —
  it returns `{ ok, message }` (the worker still raises it to the UI as an error
  banner, exactly as `resignPlayer` does), because an offer going dead under you
  is an ordinary race, not an invariant violation.

Pinned by `src/engine/career/tradeOfferValidity.test.ts` (7 tests).
